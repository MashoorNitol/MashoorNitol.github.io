About

I am a computational materials scientist with 8+ years of experience building machine-learning interatomic potentials and the HPC workflows around them. My work connects first-principles and classical atomistic codes (VASP, LAMMPS) with AI surrogates such as MACE, moment tensor potentials, and neural-network potentials.

At Los Alamos National Laboratory I led ML potential development for Ti, Sn, Pu, and refractory multi-component alloys, including the LAMMPS implementation of a neural-network potential and large-scale molecular-dynamics campaigns on DOE supercomputers. At Discovery2scale I now architect AI-driven materials-discovery software: fine-tuned MACE models and GPU pipelines for polymer crystal-structure prediction (CATENA), and a domain language model pretrained from scratch on the alloy literature (AlloyBERT).

Selected projects

CATENA

AI-guided polymer crystal-structure prediction · Discovery2scale

Multi-stage pipeline — chemistry GNN prior, helix enumeration, periodic packing, MACE pre-screening, refinement, and fine-tuned rescoring — that delivers five ranked candidate CIFs per polymer for DFT validation, with frozen blind benchmarks.

AlloyBERT

Domain language model for alloy design · Discovery2scale

DeBERTa-v3 encoder pretrained from scratch on a 70k+ paper alloy corpus, with a containerized multi-GPU training stack, an evaluation harness against SciBERT, MatSciBERT, and SteelBERT, and a downstream extraction and design loop.

ML potentials for high-entropy alloys

Los Alamos National Laboratory

ML and modified-EAM potentials for the VNbTaTiZr refractory HEA and the Ti–Al–V system, fitted to DFT and validated on deformation mechanisms, phase stability, and phase-diagram fidelity.

ML potentials for Ti, Sn, and Pu

LANL · ASC Physics and Engineering Models

MTP, neural-network, and hybrid potentials with active-learning data generation and HPC MD pipelines for phase stability and shock response.

Process–structure mapping

LANL LDRD 20250637DI

Billion-parameter variational autoencoder mapping MD-generated microstructures to processing conditions (ICCV 2025).

RANN in LAMMPS

Mississippi State University

Co-developed the rapid artificial neural network interatomic potential and its LAMMPS implementation, pair_style rann.
